# Sample Document 3: Dense Retrieval and Embedding Models

## Introduction to Dense Retrieval

Dense retrieval uses neural network-based embeddings to represent queries and documents in a continuous vector space. Unlike traditional sparse methods like BM25, dense retrieval captures semantic relationships between text segments.

## How Dense Retrieval Works

### Embedding Generation
1. A pre-trained transformer encoder (e.g., BERT, Sentence-BERT) processes text
2. The model produces a fixed-size dense vector representation
3. Similar texts have vectors that are close in the embedding space

### Similarity Search
- Query embedding is compared against document embeddings using cosine similarity
- Top-k most similar documents are retrieved
- Ranking is based on semantic similarity, not keyword frequency

## Popular Embedding Models

### Sentence-BERT (SBERT)
- Specifically designed for semantic similarity tasks
- Produces sentence-level embeddings
- All-MiniLM-L6-v2: Fast, lightweight, 384 dimensions
- all-mpnet-base-v2: Higher quality, 768 dimensions, slower

### BGE (BAAI General Embedding)
- State-of-the-art performance on benchmarks
- Available in various sizes (base, large)
- Strong multilingual support

### Instructor (HXDG)
- Task-aware embeddings
- Can be customized for specific domains

## Advantages of Dense Retrieval

1. **Semantic Understanding**: Captures meaning beyond keyword matching
2. **Generalization**: Handles synonyms and paraphrases
3. **Cross-lingual**: Some models support cross-lingual retrieval
4. **Compact**: Fixed-size representations enable efficient indexing

## Limitations

1. **Computational Cost**: Neural encoding is slower than BM25
2. **Training Dependency**: Performance depends on training data coverage
3. **Memory**: Dense indices can be larger than sparse indices
4. **Hot Tokens**: Some tokens may be underrepresented in training

## Hybrid Retrieval

Many production systems combine dense and sparse retrieval:

- **Cascade Approach**: Run both, merge results
- **Dense First**: Use dense retrieval, rerank with sparse features
- **Late Fusion**: Combine scores from both methods

## Evaluation Metrics

Common metrics for dense retrieval:
- Recall@K: Fraction of relevant documents in top-K
- MRR (Mean Reciprocal Rank): Position of first relevant document
- NDCG@K: Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain
- Hit Rate: Whether any relevant document appears in top-K

## Conclusion

Dense retrieval has become a cornerstone of modern RAG systems, enabling semantic understanding that was not possible with traditional keyword-based methods. The choice of embedding model significantly impacts system performance and should be selected based on specific task requirements.
